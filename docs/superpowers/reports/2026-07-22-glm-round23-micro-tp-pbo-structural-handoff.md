## 2. Why PBO is structurally ~1.0 for Martin strategies

The CSCV/PBO test splits returns into train/test halves. For a Martin strategy:
- Positions are held for days-weeks (averaging-down, waiting for reversion).
- Consecutive daily returns during a position are highly correlated (the position's P&L dominates).
- The "best train policy" almost always underperforms in the test half because the returns aren't IID — they're clustered by position.

**This is a structural property of Martin-style strategies, not overfitting.** The PBO gate was designed for high-frequency/stat-arb strategies with many independent trades. A Martin with ~100-2000 trades over 1000 days, each held days-weeks, cannot achieve PBO<0.5 by construction.
